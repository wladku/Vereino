## Grant settlement

- It takes the administrator a lot of time to close. "to nie jest takie zero-jedynkowe, bezinterpretacyjne" — "it isn't black and white; it's open to interpretation".
- **Rules differ per agreement.** In one example, the grant covers June to November but the money arrives in September. Some agreements allow costs from June; others only from when the money arrives.
- **Allocations can be rejected.** The club decides which part of an invoice goes to which part of a municipal grant (e.g. infrastructure). The municipality can then reject this in several points.
- **Who does the work.** For the municipal grants he and the administrator sit together: he keeps an Excel sheet "so as not to make a mistake", while she writes and stamps the invoices. For the municipal ones that means "kilka dobrych popołudniów" — "several good afternoons". The other grants are the administrator's alone, and he sees she spends "kupę czasu" — "a heap of time" on them.
- **The substantive part.** A settlement is not only financial. Each grant has its own requirements: photos, descriptions, proving that the project succeeded, and explaining why things were bought.
- **The application.** The administrator also writes the application (the "offer").
- **Records.** Documents are kept for audits, "do 5 lat … chyba" — "up to 5 years … I think".
- **Fear of error.** Even when the club acts honestly, there is fear that a bad description is read as fraud or that money is clawed back. He heard the same from other associations, including rural women's circles, at a local meeting of associations he attended. That is his account of what others said.
- **Personal liability.** For an expected grant (an exercise area and a shed), he will sign a blank promissory note guaranteeing with his personal assets.
- **Material offered.** He offered to share a sample grant agreement and settlement file. He added that it has to be seen as a whole, not as one sample, because the order of allocation matters (equipment from a given grant first).
- **The interviewer's idea.** Władek proposed a tool that takes the month's invoices with each grant's rules, suggests an optimal allocation ("opis analityczny" — "analytical description", as in his own company) and drafts the settlement document.
  - The coordinator's reaction was ambiguous: "zawsze coś tam wypluje z tego. No pytanie, czy… no nie, no chyba nie" — "it'll always spit something out. The question is whether… no, I guess not".
  - He then moved on to offering the sample.

## Attendance

- **Attendance list.** A physical attendance list is an obligation the club commits to. "lista obecności musi być" — "there has to be an attendance list".
- **Why numbers matter.** Knowing the number in advance matters to him and the coaches. In his own group he plans a session for at least 6 children; recently, with illness, 4 came, and he had to change the whole session on the spot.
- **What he would want (stated preference).**
  - For regular training, guardians report only absence: "potwierdzać nieobecność".
  - For friendlies and matches, they confirm presence. He also noted that confirming every time can be a burden for parents.
- **The last season.** The attendance list was a shared Google sheet: the coaches knew it and he saw who came. Coaches also noted fee payments in the sheet's remarks, and he summed them per group, then added who paid in cash.
- **Now.** A template where monthly bank transfers are typed in by hand from the account; at month end cash payments are added, "i wychodzi, że ktoś zapłacił, albo ktoś nie zapłacił" — "and you see who paid and who didn't".

## Urgent changes

- The only way today is the Messenger groups.
- SMS to all assigned guardians might get more attention, "chociaż też różnie to bywa" — "though that varies too".

## Call-ups

- In all age groups everyone is always called up; he called everyone in his group, and so do the other groups he knows of.
- Squads are "na styku" — "on the edge". The team playing 11-a-side would ideally travel with 15 players, but sometimes goes with 13 or exactly 11 because of illness, trips and injuries.

## ProTrainUp, as he remembers testing it

- **What it had:** a "call up for the match" feature that sends guardians a request to confirm; one calendar; membership fees; attendance per month.
- **What it lacked:** it was designed for the academy only, with no pitch or Orlik bookings and no other settings, and it is not visible on the web.
- **Price:** a fixed monthly charge. He recalled about 6,000 (currency and period not stated), with an extra charge above a player limit. This is his recollection, not a checked price.

## The club's own plans (as he described them)

- **Orlik schedule.** The Orlik management agreement the club will sign requires the club to make the Orlik schedule public, a "harmonogram Orlika". He sees it as an active calendar where people can book, so that fewer people phone the club.
- **A system being built for the club.** A person is building a website and system for the club, paid by the club, with a small fee afterwards for upkeep. He expects it soon, "za niedługo postawimy stronę" — "we'll launch the site soon". It is meant to present the coaches (with photos taken for it), each group and contact details; hold documents, declarations and GDPR consents to download; allow "Zapisz dziecko do szkółki" ("enrol a child in the academy"); and cover sign-up for the cheerleading, chess and zumba groups. He contrasts it with ProTrainUp: "krojone jest jakby pod nas" — "tailored to us".
- **Unclear from the transcript.** It is not clear whether "ta aplikacja" ("this app") in his sentences means Vereino or the system that person is building.
- **Clarified by Władek Kuć after the call (2026-10-23):** it is a separate project. The club's website is built by another person, independently of Vereino. `[DOCUMENT]` owner's statement in the om-discover session.
