# Interview — academy coordinator, 2026-10-23

Conducted in Polish. Quotes are kept in the original language with an English rendering. Names of coaches, staff and other people are replaced by roles.

## Limits of this account

- One person, describing his own work. Statements about coaches ("the coaches say it is a mess") are his account of what they say.
- The call ended before the questions on attendance, urgent changes, visibility of call-ups, tools, rollout and the counting commitment. Those remain open.
- Pay amounts he stated are deliberately not recorded: the repository is public, and no explicit rates are kept in it.

## His role, as he described it

- He coordinates the coaches, the training sessions, tournaments and matches, the training locations, and equipment.
- He manages substitutions.
- He calculates the coaches' pay at the end of each month.
- He collects the cash that coaches take from parents.
- With the administrator, he keeps the club's settlements: the cash book, bank movements and grant settlements.

## Coach pay and substitutions

- **Monthly summary.** At the end of the month every coach sends him a summary: absences, substitutions taken, and extra matches or tournaments attended. He then calculates each coach's pay.
- **Base pay.** Each coach has a fixed monthly base. The older league groups (the older under-13 group and the under-15 group) have a higher base than the younger groups. The amounts are not recorded here.
- **Absence and substitution.** An absence from one training session reduces the base by a fixed amount per session. The coach who substitutes receives that amount: "na zasadzie przeniesienia" — "as a transfer".
- **Away trips.** An away trip pays a fixed amount.
- **Finding a substitute.** Coaches first look for a substitute among themselves and go to him only if they cannot find one. "tylko ja mam wiedzieć o tym, że jest zastępstwo" — "I just have to know that there is a substitution". If nobody can cover, he runs the session himself if he can, or cancels it and moves it to another day.
- **Frequency.** Substitutions are rare now. They piled up while coaches attended a course and are more common around June and the holidays. He keeps no spreadsheet for them, because there are few.
- **Disputes.** No dispute about substitutions was described.

## Cash from parents, referees and kits

- **Paying fees.** Parents pay membership fees to the club's account or in cash to the coach.
- **Coaches handing over cash.** Coaches settle the cash with him once a month, at the end of the month, in cash. Each coach lists who paid, how much and for what (membership fee or kit), on an envelope or a sheet of paper.
- **Referees.** Coaches of the two older league groups pay referees at home league matches directly, in cash, from the collected fees or their own money. The referee issues a declaration, which serves as the payment confirmation and is used for the tax office and social-insurance payments. The coach hands over the cash and the declarations together with the settlement. Friendlies usually have no referee.
- **Recording.** He records cash in a notebook and in an Excel cash settlement. Bank account movements also go into Excel, categorised, "żebyśmy mogli ... na co po prostu wydajemy pieniądze, tak realnie" — "so we can see what we actually spend money on".
- **Accounting.** Each coach has a civil-law contract and issues a bill each month. Once a month the administrator collects all invoices, bills and declarations and takes them to the external accountant, who enters them into a simple accounting system the club pays for.

## Facilities and schedules

- **Summer.** The academy trained on one pitch. On Mondays there were five sessions, so each coach got a zone of the pitch.
- **Winter.** The academy trains in rented school halls in three nearby villages, plus the Orlik while the weather allows, with backup hall bookings in case of snow or frost. The schedule is agreed with the coaches. One coach runs two groups, and his times must not overlap.
- **Hall billing.**
  - For one hall, the club keeps a log of the hours used, and the school bills the club from that log.
  - The other two schools keep their own entry and exit logs and send monthly bills.
  - All of this is under signed contracts with the schools.
- **Training timetable.** There are no collisions in the regular training timetable. He and the administrator set it, and it is published as a summer poster and a winter poster: "tu nie może być żadnej kolizji" — "there can be no collision here".
- **Matches, tournaments and pitch maintenance collide "very often".**
  - Line painting, mowing, aeration, leaf clearing, irrigation and the turf brushing needed every 10–15 hours of use are planned by the person who maintains the pitch. Coaches do not know when they can book.
  - His example: someone planned line painting for the morning without him knowing, while a tournament was set for 9:30 and a match for 16:00.
  - He wants a separate calendar for matches, friendlies, tournaments and maintenance, which the coaches and the maintenance person can access.
- **Notice periods.** League matches are known up to two weeks ahead. Tournaments are known one or two weeks ahead, sometimes only a few days, depending on how the coaches agree among themselves.

## Rentals (how he imagines them)

He sees the Orlik and the adjacent pitch as partly commercial:
1. The club enters its own sessions, friendlies and maintenance first.
2. The free blocks are offered for rent on the website, with the regulations and rules on who has priority.
3. An outsider makes a preliminary booking.
4. A club administrator confirms the booking within about two days.
5. Fees are charged.

This is his expectation, not a description of today.

## Grant settlements (his answer to "what takes most of your time")

- The club settles several grants. "w tym roku to może było z 12" — "this year maybe about 12". They include the municipality grant, the "Klub 2024" programme, a certification-related grant, camp funding, and a ministry grant for a tournament in November.
- Some grants run all year, some half a year. Each has its own rules, for example a cap per coach or per hour.
- **Allocating invoices.** Each invoice is allocated fully or partly to one or more grants.
  - Invoices are downloaded from KSeF, the national e-invoicing system.
  - Each one is stamped and described with the amount charged to each grant, and signed.
  - It is entered in a monthly table (Excel or Word) with the invoice number, purpose, receipt date, payment date and the amount per grant, together with scans.
  - A final report on whether the goals were met goes to the municipality with signatures.
- **Risk.** The same invoice must never be claimed twice, in two grant settlements. "Moglibyśmy stracić całą dotację" — "we could lose the whole grant".
- **No later correction.** An allocation is recorded as it happens and cannot be changed afterwards.

## Equipment

- Each coach has group-specific equipment: balls and bibs sized for the age group. The rest is shared.
- He says the coaches describe the current organisation as "bałagan" — "a mess". Two options are being considered: labelling equipment per group, or numbered sets with fixed places on shelves.
- Equipment is moving to a new building, and shelving is being bought.

## Coach meetings and training plans (his intentions)

- He wants regular, in-person coach meetings. Two online evening meetings were held; the coaches said they would prefer meeting in person.
- He wants to introduce training plans ("konspekty"). Next year the academy may apply for the PZPN bronze star certification, which requires training to plans. He mentions a plan database (as he named it, "łączy nas trening") that publishes two plans per week per group.
- He would like a coach to mark in the calendar that a session followed a plan, with notes.

These are Claude's remarks, written from the transcript.

- **D-007 / R04.** Pay is a fixed base per coach, minus a fixed amount for each missed session, plus the same amount for each session taken over, plus a fixed amount per away trip. The spec's REQ-021 describes "base + units × rate". The unit transfer in D-007 matches, but the formula should be checked against this account. Substitutions are rare and settled without disputes, so the dispute-removal argument for D-007 has no support in this account.
- **Cash.** Coaches accepting cash, the monthly cash handover and the cash notebook are first-hand support for REQ-031 and REQ-033. Referee payments made from collected cash, with a declaration, are not in the spec.
- **Collisions.** The collision problem he describes is matches, tournaments and maintenance, not the training timetable. Maintenance scheduling is REQ-022 (Could) in the spec; internal bookings are REQ-023.
- **Club as a renter.** Rented school halls billed from hour logs make the club a renter of other facilities. The spec models the club's own facility.
- **Grants.** Grant settlement is the time sink he named, and "projects and grants" is a non-goal (N07).
- **Training plans.** Plans linked to sessions touch the non-goal on coaching tools (N01).
- These last two are product-owner decisions, not facts this note can settle.
